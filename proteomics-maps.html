Workflow

How the proteomics maps are generated

Measured proteins are grouped by Gene Ontology top-level categories, linked through the Clinical Knowledge Graph, and turned into annotatable maps. The same workflow applies to every system-focused dataset.

  1. 1

    Measured proteins

    Input: all proteins in the dataset

    Olink sub-panels are arbitrary subdivisions of the measured proteins, so they are not used as categories. All measured proteins go into the workflow.

  2. 2

    GO top-level categories

    Output: protein list per category

    Gene Ontology top-level terms are extracted and serve as the categories. Each one holds the measured proteins that belong to it.

  3. 3

    Clinical Knowledge Graph query

    Output: interaction network per category

    The proteins of each category are queried in the Clinical Knowledge Graph for reactions among them.

  4. 4

    SBML to annotatable map

    Output: Minerva map

    Each interaction network is converted to SBML (XML) and uploaded to Minerva as a map that can be annotated and overlaid with data.

Background

Why GO categories, not panels

Olink sub-panels group proteins for practical reasons, not by biology. Gene Ontology (GO) is a hierarchical vocabulary of biological processes, molecular functions and cellular components. Its top-level terms are the broadest categories, which gives biologically meaningful groups that proteins can be assigned to. A protein can belong to more than one category.

Clinical Knowledge Graph (CKG)

The CKG is an open-source platform (Python, MIT license) with two aims: to build a graph database from experimental data and public biomedical databases, and to automate knowledge discovery on that graph. In its original description it holds more than 16 million nodes and 220 million relationships, drawn from sources such as UniProt, STRING, IntAct, Reactome, SIGNOR and CORUM, plus ontologies including GO. Here it is used as the source of known reactions among the proteins of each category.

SBML

The Systems Biology Markup Language (SBML) is an XML-based exchange format for models of biological processes. Converting the networks to SBML makes them readable by standard systems biology tools.

Minerva

Minerva is a web platform for visualizing and annotating molecular maps. Uploaded maps can be searched, annotated and combined with overlay data, the same way as the post-COVID disease map.
