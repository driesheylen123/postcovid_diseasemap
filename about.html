Background

A large subgroup of post-COVID patients experiences dysautonomia, post-exertional malaise (PEM), fatigue and cognitive problems without a clear cause. We propose that these symptoms result from persistent neuroinflammation, disruption of the blood-brain barrier (BBB), and dysregulation of the autonomic nervous system (ANS), the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and the immune system.

Because post-COVID is a systemic disease, integrating knowledge into one comprehensive disease map is crucial to study how these systems interact.

Objectives

O1

Understand how neuroinflammation links to BBB disruption and to dysregulation of the ANS, HPA axis and immune system, including viral persistence.

O2

Follow imaging-based estimates of neuroinflammation over time and relate them to the course of symptoms.

O3

Identify imaging and molecular biomarker signatures that distinguish patients from healthy controls.

How the map is built

The disease map links multi-scale computational models of molecular pathways, cellular interactions and brain networks. It is built in Minerva on open community standards (WikiPathways, Disease Maps), so others can reuse, extend or contribute.

Data and analysis

The study includes 45 post-COVID patients (40 adults and 5 aged 16-18) and 20 matched controls from the VeCosCO, DECI-LOCO and COVAS cohorts. Each participant contributes questionnaires, MRI and [18F]FDG PET brain and body imaging, and blood profiling (immune cell phenotyping, viral persistence, proteomics, monocyte epigenetics). For VeCosCO participants, longitudinal neuroimaging is available.

Mapping patient and control data onto the disease map, with pathway- and network-based analysis, tests and improves the map and highlights differences between patients and controls and between subgroups (e.g. sex, age, severity).

Status

We are finalizing the first version of the map, covering the whole-body layer, central nervous system, immune system and metabolism. Inclusion of participants is underway and the first measurements are done; this data will be integrated into the map. Next we will add detail on other systems (vascular, muscle, mitochondrial dysfunction) and extend to other post-acute infection syndromes.

Patient participation

A patient panel receives progress reports every two months and joins project team meetings every six months.
